Technical brief to request a development proposal from Transight  
Executive summary  
The requested system is a complementary cabinet -level layer for Smartec. It must supervise and 
remotely control the main contactor and feeder contactors, provide operational-grade energy 
monitoring, detect cabinet intrusion and expose cabinet status and alarms through open APIs.  
The cable -theft workflow should correlate door opening, tamper, loss of cabinet communication, 
loss of feeder electrical continuity, simultaneous node outages and video evidence. The nearby 
security camera should therefore remain independent from the targeted cabinet, preferably through 
solar power plus battery autonomy.  
Smartec should be able to receive telemetry, alarms and camera evidence, open live video on 
demand, and automatically show the camera stream or clip when a critical alarm is triggered, while 
the camera keeps recording continuously for later review.

This note is intended to help Transight understand the concept that should be developed as a 
complementary subsystem to the Smartec platform. The objective is to supervise public lighting control panels, control main and feeder contactors remotely, obtain cabinet- level electrical data and create a 
reliable workflow for cable theft detection.  
The requested system should be designed as an open solution. Smartec must remain able to receive alarms, status data, energy information, cabinet connectivity status and camera evidence through APIs or standard interfaces, so that the customer sees one operational environment instead of several 
disconnected systems. 
2. Background and operating context  
Smartec already provides luminaire -level intelligence. Each node can report consumption data obtained 
from the DALI driver installed in the luminaire. However, the customer is now requesting an additional layer 
of monitoring at the public lighting control panel, where the outgoing feeders are switched and where a cable theft event usually begins.  
A typical public lighting control panel in this project is expected to have one main contactor and 
approximately seven outgoing lines. Each line may feed around 15 to 30 luminaires, depending on 
luminaire power, cable sizing and network configuration. For that reason, a feeder- level issue can affect a 
large number of luminaires at once and should be visible immediately from the platform.  
Key design principle: panel -level monitoring should complement luminaire -level monitoring. It should 
not duplicate the Smartec node functionality, but add a second and more resilient layer for supervision, 
alarm correlation, remote switching and incident verification.  
3. Target technical concept  
The concept is to retrofit or equip the public lighting cabinet with a controller able to monitor the main contactor, each feeder contactor and the electrical state of the cabinet. Where required, existing 
contactors can be replaced with DALI- compatible devices already available on the market, provided that 
the final solution remains fully compatible with Smartec and its DALI communication environment.  
Attached Image 1 illustrates the proposed structure, where the luminaires are intelligently controlled 
through Smartec ®, complemented by an additional smart system for control panel management. We also 
propose installing a camera powered by a solar panel and battery to provide extra surveillance for the system. 
 
Illustrative concept: The lighting system has two levels of control to enhance its reliability: each luminaire is monitored individually,

Recommended architecture at cabinet level:  
• DALI -compatible switching interface for the main contactor and feeder contactors.  
• Auxiliary feedback from contactors so the system can verify actual state rather than only the issued 
command.  
• Operational -grade total panel metering together with feeder current, voltage presence or equivalent 
feeder -health supervision.  
• Door sensor, tamper signal and controller heartbeat so Smartec receives an alarm when the cabinet is 
opened, manipulated or disconnected from communications.  
• Small backup power element for the controller so it can send a final power- loss or last -gasp event if 
the cabinet is disconnected.  
• Nearby security camera with solar panel plus battery autonomy and open video integration into 
Smartec. 
• Independent or resilient communications path, preferably smartec gateway, to maintain alarm 
reporting even when the cabinet is under attack.

6. Camera requirement near the cabinet 
A security camera should be proposed as part of the solution and installed on a nearby pole with direct 
visibility of the control panel. The key requirement is that this camera must remain operational even if the 
public lighting cabinet is disconnected, one feeder cable is cut or the cabinet is deliberately attacked.  
For this reason, the preferred design is a solar- powered camera with local battery autonomy. This point is 
important because if the cabinet or feeder supply is interrupted, a cabinet -powered camera may fail at 
exactly the moment when visual verification is most needed.  
• Acceptable day and night visibility, sufficient to identify movement and confirm that someone is 
manipulating the cabinet or nearby infrastructure.  
• Continuous recording, preferably 24 hours a day, so operators can review the period before and after 
an event.  
• Live video view available inside Smartec on demand.  
• Automatic opening of live video, snapshot or event clip inside Smartec when a critical cabinet alarm is 
triggered.  
• Open video integration, ideally through ONVIF, RTSP or equivalent API access to snapshots, clips or event references. 
• Ability to send image evidence or a retrievable media reference into Smartec.  
7. Design recommendations to strengthen the proposal  
Below are practical improvements that, from a public -lighting control perspective, would make the 
concept easier to deploy and more credible for the customer:  
• Prefer DALI -2 compatible devices where available, or provide a clear compatibility statement with the 
Smartec environment.  
• Use auxiliary contacts and feeder measurements together so the platform can distinguish commanded OFF, electrical fault and possible theft or disconnection.  
• Provide a local rules engine or alarm correlation layer at edge level so several weak alarms can be 
converted into one stronger incident.  
• Include timestamped event history, communications heartbeat logs and diagnostics to support 
maintenance teams and incident investigation.  
• Keep all interfaces open and documented. The value of the system is much higher if the customer can integrate it into the existing Smartec platform rather than operate it as a separate silo.  
Important implementation note: the system should not depend exclusively on cabinet mains power at 
the exact moment when theft begins. The controller should support a last -gasp event or small backup 
supply, and the camera should have its own autonomous power source; otherwise the most critical 
alarm and the most useful video evidence may disappear at the same moment the cabinet is 
disconnected.

To make the next discussion productive, Transight should be asked to respond with both technical and 
commercial content. The proposal should ideally include:  
• High -level architecture of the cabinet solution, including controller, metering, main and feeder 
contactors, sensors, communications and camera components.  
• Explanation of how DALI compatibility with Smartec will be achieved.  
• Proposed API model for alarms, telemetry, command exchange, live video access and camera 
evidence.  
• Alarm matrix showing how a suspected cable theft event will be detected, prioritised and visualised.  
• Description of cabinet connectivity supervision, heartbeat logic and communications -loss alarm 
behaviour.  
• Description of the camera power architecture, storage or retention approach and night- visibility 
performance.  
• Indicative bill of materials and installation assumptions per cabinet.  
• Any platform software required, clearly identifying what remains open and what is proprietary.  
• Pilot deployment scope, commissioning methodology and support responsibilities.  
9. Confirmed design assumptions for proposal preparation  
The following points are now considered confirmed requirements and should be reflected in the proposal 
rather than treated as open questions:  
• Main contactor control is required, in addition to feeder contactor control.  
• Operational -grade metering is sufficient; revenue -grade metering is not required.  
• The cabinet solution must supervise communications health and generate an alarm if connectivity is 
lost.  
• Every cabinet door opening must generate an alarm, and cabinet tamper detection is strongly 
recommended.  
• Smartec should be able to open live video on demand and automatically display video, snapshot or 
clip when a critical alarm occurs.  
• The camera should record continuously so that earlier events can be reviewed after an incident.  
• The camera must provide acceptable night visibility for movement verification around the cabinet.  
• The camera power supply should be independent from the targeted cabinet, preferably by solar panel plus battery autonomy.  
With these clarifications, the brief gives Transight a more precise target: cabinet- level electrical 
supervision, remote control of main and feeder contactors, multi -source theft detection and open 
integration of alarms and video into Smartec.
